About Me

Solar After-Sales Technician & Electrical Engineering Graduate

I’m Gift Mthombeni, an Electrical Engineering graduate specializing in Power Systems, with practical experience working in the solar industry as an after-sales technician. My work involves technical support, inverter and battery fault diagnosis, system commissioning, system design modifications, customer technical advice, and hands-on troubleshooting of solar backup and hybrid energy systems.

I have worked with low-voltage battery banks and high-voltage rack battery systems up to around 400 V, including battery paralleling, BMS communication, inverter-to-battery communication, and configuration of lithium battery operating modes. I also have practical exposure to inverter paralleling and communication, including work involving 50 kW T-Rex inverter systems.

My technical experience includes resolving common inverter faults such as bus soft-start errors, communication issues, battery detection problems, configuration faults, and system behavior problems linked to output priority settings. I understand key inverter operating strategies such as Solar First, Utility First, Battery First, and hybrid combinations used to optimize backup performance and energy usage.

I also understand grid export behavior, export control, CT metering, ATS integration, online inverter monitoring and configuration platforms such as EDMS, and generator integration. This includes practical understanding of synchronizing single-phase and three-phase inverter systems with generators, including systems up to around 12 kVA.

Alongside my solar technical experience, I build software tools and dashboards using technologies such as React, Node.js, Python, MySQL, Firebase, Git, GitHub, and Render. This allows me to combine engineering knowledge with digital systems, automation, and data-driven problem-solving.

Power-Factor Correction Study

View Full Report

This study modeled a 33 kV radial feeder in MATLAB and DIgSILENT PowerFactory to evaluate the effect of capacitor-bank compensation on voltage regulation and system power factor. Adding a 0.63 MVAr capacitor improved voltage to 0.977 p.u. and raised power factor from 0.87 to 0.95 lagging, confirming reduced reactive losses.
